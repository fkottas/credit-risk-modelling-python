# Chapter 7 — Retail Credit Products

## Product structure determines the risk data

An instalment loan has a scheduled amortisation path, a credit card has a reusable limit and uncertain future drawdown, and a mortgage combines long maturity, collateral, prepayment and legal recovery. The same score cannot be transferred among these products merely because all borrowers are individuals.

For an instalment loan, application variables, amount, term, price and payment schedule are known at origination. Behavioural risk emerges through payment performance. EAD usually falls with amortisation unless arrears, fees or redraw features intervene. For a revolving facility with reference balance $B_t$, limit $L_t$ and credit conversion factor $CCF_t$, a common construction is

\[
EAD_{t+h}=B_t+CCF_t\max(L_t-B_t,0).
\]

Utilisation, payment ratio, over-limit behaviour, cash advances and recent limit changes can therefore affect both PD and EAD. A mortgage’s LGD depends on property value, lien, cure, foreclosure timing, cost and discounting; a regional house-price index cannot replace collateral-level valuation and legal data [R13–R14, R78].

The worked example implements the instalment and revolving definitions separately. It rejects an internally inconsistent limit below the drawn balance and retains the raw CCF rather than concealing an out-of-range value. Extensions introduce amortisation, fees, over-limit balances and limit changes, each with a stated reference date.

`ccf_raw` is retained even when it falls outside zero to one. A value above one may reflect limit exceedance, interest and fees, limit changes, timing mismatch or error. The modelling boundary is a separate field so the data issue is not erased.

## Product policy and segmentation

Define product eligibility, maximum term, amount or limit, affordability treatment, collateral requirements and permitted overrides before choosing a model. Monitor risk by product, vintage, channel, score band and policy version. A stable portfolio average can hide deterioration in a new digital channel.

# Chapter 8 — SME and Corporate Lending

## Obligor and facility dimensions

Corporate credit separates obligor default risk from facility loss. An obligor may have loans, guarantees, revolving lines and derivatives with different collateral, seniority and maturity. Financial statements, management quality, industry position, group support and country risk inform the rating, while facility structure informs LGD and EAD.

SMEs blur retail and corporate processes. Data may be sparse, owner and business finances intertwined, and financial statements delayed. Basel asset-class assignment is not a modelling convenience; exposure type, management and applicable rules determine the approach. Specialised lending depends primarily on cash flows generated by a project or object. Where parameter estimation does not satisfy the relevant requirements, supervisory slotting may apply [R1–R4].

The worked example derives four common ratios from stated financial-statement fields:

\[
\text{Debt/EBITDA}=\frac{\text{interest-bearing debt}}{\text{EBITDA}},\quad
\text{Interest cover}=\frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{interest expense}},
\]

\[
\text{DSCR}=\frac{\text{cash available for debt service}}{\text{principal due}+\text{interest due}},\quad
\text{Equity ratio}=\frac{\text{equity}}{\text{total assets}}.
\]

A zero or economically meaningless denominator is reported as undefined, not replaced by an invented extreme ratio. Interpretation also requires statement date, consolidation perimeter, accounting policy, one-off adjustments, cash-flow volatility and sector context.

The sales adjustment affects prescribed asset correlation in the teaching function. It does not establish SME classification, eligibility or regulatory capital by itself.

## Rating policy

A corporate rating policy should define rating philosophy, group treatment, expert judgement, overrides, review frequency, watchlist, default, cure and data precedence. Override direction and reason must be stored. A model output that is routinely overridden may indicate a missing risk driver, policy inconsistency or weak use test.

# Chapter 9 — Digital Credit and Embedded Finance

## Fast decisions do not remove credit risk

BNPL and embedded credit reduce application friction and may rely on small tickets, short horizons, merchant data and repeated transactions. These characteristics change the modelling unit and data latency; they do not eliminate affordability, fraud, adverse selection or customer-treatment risk. Multiple small facilities can accumulate into material exposure.

A transaction-level label can leak repeat-customer information if the same person appears in both train and test. Split by customer and time. Merchant, device and behavioural variables may be predictive but can create privacy, proxy and stability concerns. Thin files require uncertainty-aware decisions, lower initial limits or additional evidence rather than an artificially precise PD.

The worked example builds a BNPL schedule from purchase price, instalment count and verified monthly income. If instalment $k$ is $I_k$ and verified monthly net income is $Y$, the simple payment burden is $\sum_{k\in m}I_k/Y$ for payments due in month $m$. This ratio is only a starting point: housing costs, essential expenditure, existing commitments, income volatility and concurrent facilities determine whether it is meaningful. The broader origination process and affordability assessment require policy and jurisdiction-specific treatment [R13].

Daily aggregation in this short example is descriptive. A real process needs merchant, customer and cohort views, mature labels, fraud separation, repeat-use exposure and limit history.

## Embedded-credit policy

Record who is lender of record, which party owns the model, who supplies data, how consent and notices work, and which party handles disputes. A merchant optimisation objective must not silently become the credit-risk objective. Monitor approval, take-up, default, refund, dispute and repeat-borrowing outcomes.

# Chapter 10 — Portfolio Segmentation and Sparse Defaults

## Segmentation is a modelling hypothesis

Prime and subprime are not universal score thresholds. They are portfolio concepts defined by risk appetite, product, market and time. Thin-file means limited relevant information, not automatically high or low risk. Low-default portfolios, common in sovereign, bank or high-quality corporate lending, have too few events for unconstrained estimation.

Segmentation can improve within-group homogeneity but reduces the sample available to estimate each model. Define candidate segments from product structure, underwriting and population differences before examining performance, then test whether relationships, calibration and decisions differ out of time. A segment with zero observed defaults does not have zero PD. With $n$ independent observations and no defaults, the approximate 95% upper confidence bound is $3/n$ (the “rule of three”), illustrating how slowly uncertainty declines in a sparse sample.

The Polish bankruptcy data provide a useful low-event modelling lab, but bankruptcy is not identical to regulatory default and the dataset lacks observation dates. Its use must remain scoped.

The worked example implements a segmentation rule using bureau depth, open trades, an estimated risk indicator and the amount of default evidence. Its thresholds are illustrative and must be replaced by a documented portfolio definition. The Polish bankruptcy case is used later to study rare events, but its outcome remains bankruptcy rather than bank regulatory default. Low-default estimation and margins of conservatism require additional evidence under the applicable IRB framework [R4, R10].

For thin or low-default data, consider conservative pooling, external evidence with documented comparability, Bayesian priors, rating-level calibration and margins of conservatism. External data cannot be pasted onto the portfolio without mapping definition, geography, cycle and underwriting.

## Segment governance

Maintain segment assignment code, rationale, owners and change history. Monitor population shares and migrations. If a new channel shifts many cases into thin-file status, determine whether the change is real or a data-feed failure.

**Lab.** Compare a pooled model with separate prime and subprime models. Use an out-of-time sample and report performance plus confidence intervals. Explain when poorer segment-level AUC may still accompany better calibration or policy control.

# Chapter 11 — The Credit Account Lifecycle

## One borrower produces several modelling datasets

Origination captures information available before the initial decision. Account management uses updated balances, payment behaviour and limit history. Collections begins before or after contractual delinquency according to policy. Default applies a formal definition and reference date. Recovery records cash flows, costs, collateral actions, cure and closure. Mixing fields among stages creates leakage.

A recovery dataset should contain one row per cash flow or a traceable aggregation. EAD, effective interest rate and default date must remain consistent within an account. Cash flows before default require explanation. Closed workouts and incomplete workouts must be distinguished because later recoveries are censored.

The worked example represents allowed lifecycle transitions as a state-transition table and checks each event in order. An invalid transition, such as application directly to recovery, is reported for investigation instead of being assigned a guessed state. Event-effective time, processing time, correction version and balance reconciliation are then added before a recovery model is constructed. This separation is necessary because IFRS 9 staging, regulatory default and operational collections can use related events without having identical definitions [R4–R6].

The synthetic cash-flow history is designed for unrestricted exercises. It omits legal and operational complexity and therefore cannot estimate a real recovery policy.

## Lifecycle controls

Create a canonical event taxonomy: due, paid, missed, delinquent, restructured, forborne, defaulted, cured, written off, sold and closed. Define precedence when states conflict. Store event-effective and system-recorded timestamps. Reconcile balances at every handoff.

**Lab.** Trace five fields—income, utilisation, days past due, collateral value and recovery cash flow—through the lifecycle. For each, state the earliest time it can lawfully and logically enter a model.

# Chapter 12 — Credit Policy, Affordability, and Overrides

## Model risk and policy risk are different

A PD model estimates risk under its data-generating process. Credit policy decides which applications to accept, at what amount and terms, subject to affordability, law, operations, concentration and strategy. A cutoff is therefore a policy parameter informed by model output and economics. It is not an intrinsic property of AUC.

For an application with performing margin $M$, EAD $E$, LGD $L$ and PD $p$, a simplified expected value is

\[
EV(p)=(1-p)M-pLE=M-p(M+LE).
\]

Setting $EV(p)=0$ gives the break-even probability

\[
p^*=\frac{M}{M+LE}.
\]

The derivation shows why a cutoff is an economic-policy choice rather than a property of AUC. Funding, capital, operations, early repayment, acquisition, fraud, taxes and customer-treatment constraints change the objective [R78].

The worked example creates an immutable `PolicyDecision` record and a small `apply_credit_policy` function. Affordability, model appetite and delegated amount produce separate reason codes and a human-review flag. The policy does not alter the estimated PD, and “eligible for human approval” remains distinct from an executed customer decision. Later chapters estimate cutoff economics after selection bias and validation have been introduced.

Realised profit in a historical accepted sample is affected by selection and does not reveal outcomes for rejected applications. Policy simulation should distinguish predictive uncertainty, reject bias and causal effects of amount, price or treatment.

## Override policy

Define permitted override types, authority, evidence, maximum frequency and monitoring. Track model score, model grade, policy result, final decision and reason separately. A manual decline based on verified fraud evidence is different from changing PD. An override that affects a protected group disproportionately requires investigation even if aggregate default falls.
