## Model and AI risk

- Federal Reserve, [SR 26-2 - Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm), 17 April 2026.
- OCC, [Bulletin 2026-13 - Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-13.html), 17 April 2026.
- FDIC, [FIL-15-2026 - Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management](https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2026/revised-guidance-model-risk-management), 17 April 2026.
- NIST, [AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework).
- European Union, [consolidated Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as at 27 July 2026](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727).
- CJEU, [Case C-634/21, SCHUFA Holding (Scoring)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62021CJ0634), 7 December 2023.

The EU AI Act classifies specified AI uses that evaluate natural persons' creditworthiness or establish their credit score as high-risk, subject to scope and the fraud-detection exception. Under the consolidated text reviewed on 22 August 2026, Chapter III Sections 1–3 for Annex III systems apply from 2 December 2027. Prudential internal models used to calculate capital requirements are treated differently. The book therefore does not collapse origination credit scoring, IFRS 9 estimation, and IRB capital modelling into one legal category.

The April 2026 US model-risk guidance expressly excludes generative- and agentic-AI models from its scope. That exclusion is not permission for autonomous credit action. The book applies separate AI-risk, information-security, consumer-law, evidence, permission and human-approval controls to those systems.
